How the queue is built

  1. Base score — the dataset's Overall score (0–5), the plain average of the ten assessment dimensions. We use it unchanged because it is simple to explain and defend.
  2. Red flags — each flag subtracts 0.5 points. The result is the adjusted score.
  3. Strict rule — a project with a 0 in any dimension can never be placed in "Review now", whatever its adjusted score. This is a minimum condition, not a penalty.
  4. Ranking — active projects are sorted by adjusted score. Ties are broken by: higher overall score → fewer red flags → higher lowest-dimension score → project ID.
  5. Grouping — ranks 1–10 (eligible only) = Review now (this month); next ranks up to 50 = Review later (next quarter); the rest = Not ready (ask for more information). Completed projects are listed last because they need no investment review.

Red flags and why they matter

  • Early stage — status Concept or In planning: nothing has been implemented or validated yet, so scores are based on intentions.
  • Serious risk — key risk is Funding uncertainty, Regulatory approval delays or Maintenance costs: risks that directly threaten a fund's capital or the project's long-term viability.
  • Big budget + early stage — budget above €20M while still Concept / In planning: large exposure with little evidence. (Such projects also get the early-stage flag, so −1.0 in total.)
  • Weak spot — any of the ten dimensions scored 1 or lower: a good average can hide a serious weakness.

Data note

  • Source: VB_Coding_JuSIIIIA_General_Project_Dataset_250_EN.xlsx (250 constructed project profiles; no missing values).
  • Used: status, sector, policy goals, budget, key risk, the ten dimension scores and the overall score. Other columns (description, location, beneficiaries, technology, duration, etc.) are not used for prioritisation and are left out of the dashboard.
  • Illustrative only: titles, budgets and scores are classroom examples. Every Assessment note reads "Illustrative project profile…", so there is no real evidence text to check.
  • Missing: real financial returns, evidence documents, who scored the projects and how, and more than one risk per project.

Limitations & responsible use

  • The −0.5 penalty, the €20M threshold and the list of "serious" risks are team judgements, not validated weights. Use the rules panel above to test how sensitive the queue is.
  • Projects with near-identical scores can land on either side of a group boundary; the tiebreak is mechanical. Look at the neighbours of the 10th and 50th project before deciding.
  • A place in the queue is a suggestion for what to review first. Final investment decisions need human due diligence and committee accountability.
